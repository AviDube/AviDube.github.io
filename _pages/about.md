## Information

Hey! I'm Avi — a Computer Engineering Master's student at **Carnegie Mellon University** and a Drone Researcher at **CMU AirLab**. My research sits at the intersection of physical AI and autonomous systems: integrating large language models, transformers, and vision systems to build robots that can reason about and act in the real world.

At AirLab I'm enhancing **AirStack** — an open-source drone simulation platform built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim — and building a drone solution to autonomously monitor excavation progress for Japanese construction firm **Shimizu**. Before CMU, I founded **Codex Laboratories** and created **SWARM**, a cloud-based multi-agent drone simulation platform adopted by MIT Lincoln Laboratory and research institutions worldwide.

Over three summers at **Saab**, I went from building acoustic sensor fusion models and Kafka data pipelines (2023), to designing photo-realistic Unreal Engine environments and computer vision systems for the US Navy's TSUNOMI project (2024), to training a **YOLOv11** object detection model on 10,000 synthetic images at **89% precision** and engineering a real-time asynchronous detection pipeline in Unreal Engine with C++ (2025). This past summer I was at **Aurora Flight Sciences (A Boeing Company)**, extending their DARPA-funded **FALCON** adaptive control framework from boats to quadrotors — building a swappable PX4 rate-controller library, tuning an adaptive observer-based controller, and designing closed-form control barrier functions for safe guidance — culminating in a live flight demonstration for DARPA.

One highlight from CMU: as part of the Large Air Vehicle Development Course, our team designed, built, and flew the **largest drone ever constructed at Carnegie Mellon** — a dodecacopter with a custom carbon fiber airframe weighing ~140 lbs and delivering ~600 lbs of thrust. Seeing it fly after 5–6 hour days in the lab made every late night worth it.

When I'm not in lab or doing homework, you'll find me on the basketball court.
